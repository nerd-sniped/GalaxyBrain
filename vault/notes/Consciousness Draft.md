# Consciousness — Draft

> **Status:** Very rough draft. Not ready to publish.

This note explores the **hard problem of consciousness** — why there is subjective experience at all, rather than just information processing in the dark.

## The Hard Problem

David Chalmers distinguishes:

- **Easy problems** — explaining cognitive functions (attention, memory, perception). Hard in practice, but in principle solvable by functional explanation.
- **Hard problem** — why is there *something it is like* to be a creature with those functions?

No amount of functional explanation tells us *why* information processing produces qualia — the felt redness of red, or the ache of longing.

## Physicalist Responses

- **Eliminativism** — qualia are an illusion; folk-psychological concepts are false (Churchland)
- **Functionalism** — mental states are defined by causal roles; substrate-independence follows
- **Higher-order theories** — a mental state is conscious when the system represents itself as being in that state

## Non-Physicalist Responses

- **Property dualism** — consciousness is a non-physical property of physical systems
- **Panpsychism** — experience is fundamental at every level of reality

## Connections
